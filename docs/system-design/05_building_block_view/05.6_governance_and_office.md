# 05.6 — Governance and Office

Owns constitutional positions, who holds them, the bodies they sit on, and
the record of decisions those bodies make.

Deliberately narrow. This context records **decisions, not process** — who
approved, when, under what authority. Elections, ballots, meetings, agendas
and minutes are out of scope (ADR-072).

---

## 5.6.1 Why this context exists at all

It would be reasonable to argue that a membership platform has no business
knowing what a board is. Two earlier decisions make it unavoidable:

```txt
ADR-046   membership admission requires approval by a governing body
          → the system must resolve "the board" to actual people

ADR-051   governance rights are a separate entitlement axis
          → those rights must point at something
```

Once approval routing has to resolve to real humans holding real positions
with real terms, the alternative to modelling office is hard-coding a
`board_approver` role and hoping every tenant's constitution fits it. It
does not.

---

## 5.6.2 Responsibilities

### Owns

- `Office` — a constitutional position
- `OfficeHolding` — a person occupying it, for a term
- `Committee` — a named body with defined authority
- `CommitteeMembership`
- `DecisionRecord` — the auditable act of a body deciding something

### Does not own

- Roles or permissions (Authorization) — an office _confers_ a role
- Governance rights on memberships (Membership, 05.3) — this context
  consumes them as eligibility input
- Elections, meetings, minutes — out of scope

---

## 5.6.3 Office is not Role

The distinction that keeps this context from collapsing into the
authorization model (ADR-071).

```txt
OFFICE     a constitutional position
           has a term, a title, governance meaning
           President, Treasurer, General Secretary

ROLE       a permission bundle
           has no term of its own, no constitutional meaning
           tenant_admin, membership_approver
```

An office **confers** roles:

```txt
Office(President) ──confers──▶ RoleDefinition(tenant_admin)
                  ──confers──▶ RoleDefinition(membership_approver)
```

One arrow, two concepts, neither collapsed into the other.

Why it matters: a role may be assigned to anyone the tenant chooses, at any
scope, for operational reasons. An office may only be held by someone
constitutionally eligible, for a bounded term, and vacating it must remove
every conferred role atomically. Merging them means either roles acquire
constitutional baggage or offices lose their term semantics.

```txt
Office
    id
    tenant_id
    org_unit_id           nullable; offices may be association-wide
                          or specific to a chapter or department
    title
    term_months           nullable
    max_consecutive_terms nullable
    eligibility_policy_id nullable → Policy
    seat_count            default 1; e.g. three Vice-Presidents
    status                ACTIVE | ABOLISHED
```

`seat_count` exists because constitutions routinely provide for multiple
holders of one office — several vice-presidents, a fixed number of elected
board members. Modelling those as distinct offices would multiply the
catalogue and lose the fact that they are the same position.

## 5.6.4 Office holding

```txt
OfficeHolding
    id
    office_id
    person_id
    seat_ordinal            1..seat_count
    valid_from
    valid_until             required; offices are MANDATORY_TERM
    holding_type            SUBSTANTIVE | ACTING
    substantive_holding_id  nullable; set when holding_type = ACTING
    decision_record_id      → DecisionRecord; how they came to hold it
    vacated_at              nullable; early vacation
    vacation_reason         RESIGNED | REMOVED | DECEASED | TERM_ENDED
```

### Terms are mandatory here

Office role definitions carry `term_policy = MANDATORY_TERM` (ADR-069). An
office holding without an end date cannot be created. This is one of the
three cases where the term is not optional, alongside break-glass grants and
cross-tenant grants.

### Expiry is evaluated at decision time

```txt
An expired holding is inert the moment it expires,
regardless of whether any sweeper has run.
```

(ADR-070) A president whose term ended at midnight cannot approve a
membership at 00:01, even if no background job has processed the expiry.
Every conferred role check consults the holding's validity window as part of
the decision.

### Acting holders

```txt
Alice   SUBSTANTIVE  President   2026-04-01 → 2028-03-31
Bob     ACTING       President   2027-06-01 → 2027-08-31
                     substantive_holding_id → Alice's holding
```

Both rows exist. Only one is effective at a time — the acting holding takes
precedence within its window, and Alice's substantive holding is not deleted
or truncated. When Bob's window closes, Alice's authority resumes
automatically.

This matters for audit: "who was President in July 2027" has two correct
answers depending on whether you mean substantively or effectively, and the
model preserves both.

### Vacating removes conferred roles atomically

```txt
OfficeVacated
    → revoke every conferred role assignment
    → SYNCHRONOUS write to OpenFGA before the vacation is effective
```

Per the revocation rule (05.0.7). A former treasurer must not retain
financial authority for the duration of an outbox dispatch.

## 5.6.5 Committees

```txt
Committee
    id
    tenant_id
    org_unit_id            nullable
    name
    kind                   STANDING | PROJECT | AD_HOC
    authority_policy_id    nullable → Policy
    quorum                 nullable; recorded, not enforced
    parent_committee_id    nullable
    status                 ACTIVE | DISSOLVED
```

```txt
CommitteeMembership
    id
    committee_id
    person_id
    role_in_committee      CHAIR | SECRETARY | MEMBER | EX_OFFICIO
    via_office_id          nullable; ex-officio seats
    valid_from
    valid_until
```

### Ex-officio membership

`via_office_id` handles the common constitutional pattern where a committee
seat belongs to whoever currently holds an office, rather than to a named
person:

```txt
Membership Committee
 ├── CHAIR       → named person
 ├── MEMBER      → named person
 └── EX_OFFICIO  → via_office_id = Office(General Secretary)
```

The seat follows the office. When the general secretary changes, the
committee membership updates without a separate act.

### Quorum is recorded, not enforced

The field exists because constitutions specify it and decisions must record
whether it was met. The platform does not run meetings, so it cannot enforce
it — it records what the body asserted.

This is the boundary of ADR-072 made concrete: the _number_ is in scope
because it appears on a decision record; the _meeting_ is not.

## 5.6.6 Decision records

The carve-out that makes ADR-072 workable: **record the decision, not the
process.**

```txt
DecisionRecord
    id
    tenant_id
    deciding_body_type      OFFICE | COMMITTEE | GENERAL_BODY
    deciding_body_id
    decision_type           MEMBERSHIP_ADMISSION | MEMBERSHIP_TERMINATION
                          | LIFE_MEMBERSHIP_CONFERRAL
                          | OFFICE_APPOINTMENT | STAY_APPROVAL
                          | AFFILIATION_SANCTION | POLICY_ADOPTION
                          | tenant-defined
    subject_type
    subject_id              what the decision is about
    outcome                 APPROVED | REJECTED | DEFERRED
    decided_at
    recorded_by_person_id
    participants []         person ids present for the decision
    quorum_met              nullable boolean
    reference               tenant's own minute or resolution number
```

### What it deliberately does not contain

```txt
NO   deliberation, discussion, dissent
NO   reasons for rejection (see 05.3.4)
NO   attachments, minutes, transcripts
```

`reference` exists so a tenant can point at its own minute book. The platform
records that a decision was made, by whom, and what it decided — enough for
audit and for downstream workflow — and nothing more.

### Decisions are the source of several facts elsewhere

```txt
DecisionRecord(MEMBERSHIP_ADMISSION)   → Membership.application decided_by
DecisionRecord(LIFE_MEMBERSHIP_CONFERRAL) → Membership.conferred_by
DecisionRecord(OFFICE_APPOINTMENT)     → OfficeHolding.decision_record_id
DecisionRecord(STAY_APPROVAL)          → Stay.approval_record_id
DecisionRecord(AFFILIATION_SANCTION)   → the sanction act in 05.1
```

Every constitutionally significant act in the system traces to a decision
record. That traceability is the reason this context is not optional.

## 5.6.7 Approval routing

How "the board approves" becomes an actual authorization check.

```txt
approve(membership_application)

  1. what permission does this require?
         membership.approve

  2. who holds it at this scope?
         role assignments at tenant or org unit
         + roles conferred by currently-valid office holdings
         + roles conferred by committee membership

  3. does the acting principal hold it?
         check(principal, membership.approve, application)

  4. record the act
         DecisionRecord with participants and outcome
```

Step 2 is why office holdings feed the authorization graph. Step 4 is why the
graph alone is insufficient — an authorization check answers _may they_, and
the decision record answers _did they, and when_.

**The platform does not know what a board is.** It knows who holds
`membership.approve` at the relevant scope. Whether that is a board of
fifteen, a three-person committee, or a general secretary acting alone is
tenant governance expressed as office and committee configuration.

## 5.6.8 Governance rights as eligibility input

Membership (05.3.3) owns whether a person may vote, stand or hold office.
This context consumes that:

```txt
appoint(person, office)
    requires  GovernanceProfile.may_hold_office on their membership
    requires  Office.eligibility_policy satisfied
    requires  max_consecutive_terms not exceeded
    produces  DecisionRecord + OfficeHolding
```

The eligibility policy is tenant-configured and may reference criteria the
platform knows nothing about. Evaluation routes through Policy (05.7) and
evidence through Verification (05.9). The platform hard-codes no
constitutional rule.

## 5.6.9 Published events

```txt
OfficeAppointed          → Authorization  (grant conferred roles)
OfficeVacated            → Authorization  (synchronous revocation)
OfficeTermExpiring       → notification, 90/30/7 days ahead
ActingHoldingStarted     → Authorization
ActingHoldingEnded       → Authorization  (synchronous)
CommitteeMemberAdded     → Authorization
CommitteeMemberRemoved   → Authorization  (synchronous)
CommitteeDissolved       → Authorization  (synchronous)
DecisionRecorded         → Membership, Resource, Organization
```

`OfficeTermExpiring` exists because expiry is enforced at decision time
(ADR-070) and will therefore be silent and abrupt without notification. A
president discovering at 00:01 that they can no longer approve anything is a
correct outcome and a bad experience.

## 5.6.10 Authorization model fragment

```txt
type office
  relations
    define tenant: [tenant]
    define org_unit: [organizational_unit]
    define holder: [person]
    define confers: [role_definition]

type committee
  relations
    define tenant: [tenant]
    define member: [person] or holder from ex_officio_office
    define chair: [person]
```

**Validity windows are not expressed in the graph.** OpenFGA holds the tuple;
the decision-time check consults PostgreSQL for the holding's window. Putting
temporal validity into the graph would require rewriting tuples on a clock,
which is exactly the sweeper dependency ADR-070 rejects.
