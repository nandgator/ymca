# 05.2 — Identity and Person

Owns the human. One `Person` record per human across the entire platform,
independent of any tenant, plus the authenticated principals they use and the
Person→Person relationships that let one human act for another.

This context is small in surface area and disproportionately consequential:
almost every privacy and data-protection obligation in the system attaches
to something defined here.

---

## 5.2.1 Responsibilities

### Owns

- `Person` — the human, global
- `Principal` — an authenticated identity bound to a Person
- `Guardianship` — Person→Person, legal
- `ConsentGrant` — tenant-scoped, purpose-bound permission to process
- `ContactPoint` — email, phone, address, with verification state

### Does not own

- Membership, employment, or any relationship to an organization — those
  belong to Membership, Governance and the Authorization graph
- Authentication itself — that is the IdP
- Verification records — those belong to Safeguarding (05.9), which this
  context consumes

## 5.2.2 Person is global

```txt
Person
    id                  opaque, stable, never reused
    given_name
    family_name
    display_name
    date_of_birth       nullable
    sex                 nullable
    preferred_language
    status              ACTIVE | DECEASED | MERGED | SCRUBBED
    merged_into_id      nullable → Person
    created_at
```

**No `tenant_id`.** A Person exists independently of every tenant. Alice is
one human whether she is a member in Bombay, an employee in Pune, or neither.

This is a deliberate and slightly uncomfortable choice for a system whose
first principle is tenant isolation, so it deserves its justification stated
plainly:

```txt
If Person were tenant-scoped:
    the same human becomes N records
    duplicate detection becomes cross-tenant data leakage
    a person moving between associations loses their history
    guardianship across tenants is unrepresentable
    "one person, one identity" (ADR-039) becomes unenforceable
```

**Isolation is preserved not by partitioning the person, but by partitioning
what may be seen about them.** A tenant may see a Person only through a
relationship it holds — its own membership, employment or guest records. The
bare existence of a Person conveys nothing.

```txt
Bombay queries Person:alice
    → sees:      her Bombay membership, her Bombay bookings
    → does not see: that she is also a member in Pune
    → does not learn: whether she exists in any other tenant
```

Practically, this means **Person lookup is never a global search.** A tenant
searches within the set of people it has a relationship with. Adding a new
person is a create-or-link operation mediated by verified contact points, not
a browse of the global directory.

### Merge and scrub

```txt
MERGED      duplicate resolution; merged_into_id points at the survivor;
            the shell persists so historical references still resolve

SCRUBBED    personal fields nulled, audit trail and structural
            references preserved (ADR-093)
```

Neither state deletes the row. Deletion would break audit chains that must
survive for governance and safeguarding reasons.

## 5.2.3 Principals

```txt
Principal
    id
    person_id           → Person
    idp_subject         the IdP's stable subject claim
    kind                PERSONAL | STAFF | ELEVATED
    label               human-readable, e.g. "work account"
    status              ACTIVE | SUSPENDED | RETIRED
    created_at
```

One Person, potentially several Principals (ADR-040). These are genuinely
distinct authenticated identities, not aliases:

```txt
Person: Bob
 ├── Principal(PERSONAL)   his member account; books squash courts
 ├── Principal(STAFF)      his work account; holds operational roles
 └── Principal(ELEVATED)   platform support; JIT access only
```

**Why this matters.** Authorization attaches to the Principal, not the
Person. Bob logged in as PERSONAL cannot exercise his support authority
however urgent the incident, and the audit record shows which identity acted.
Collapsing these would mean a support engineer's routine gym booking and a
break-glass tenant access are performed by the same subject.

**Constraints:**

- At most one `ELEVATED` principal per Person, and it holds no standing
  authority — only the capacity to receive JIT grants.
- Role assignments name a Principal. There is no "assign to the human."
- Suspending a Person suspends every Principal. Suspending one Principal
  does not affect the others.

## 5.2.4 Guardianship

The first and only Person→Person relationship in the model.

```txt
Guardianship
    id
    guardian_person_id      → Person
    minor_person_id         → Person
    relationship_type       PARENT | LEGAL_GUARDIAN | OTHER
    verification_id         → Verification (05.9)
    established_at
    ends_at                 nullable; typically the minor's majority
    revoked_at              nullable
```

### Global, like Person

Guardianship is a fact about two humans, not about a tenant. A parent is a
parent in Bombay and in Pune. It is stored once.

### Distinct from membership dependency

```txt
GUARDIANSHIP            legal      who consents, receives, acts
DEPENDENT MEMBERSHIP    commercial who is covered, who pays
```

They frequently coincide and must not be merged (ADR-042). They come apart
in every direction:

```txt
Guardian who is not a member
    a parent enrols a child and never joins

Dependent who needs no guardian
    the spouse on a couple membership

Guardian who is not the membership holder
    grandparent holds and pays; mother consents

Guardianship outliving the membership
    consent must persist after lapse, because consent is what
    made the past processing lawful
```

Merging them produces a system where a parent must buy a membership in order
to consent for their child. Wrong commercially, wrong legally.

### Verification is required

A claimed guardian is not a guardian. Every `Guardianship` carries a
`verification_id`. Unverified claims may exist as pending applications; they
confer nothing until verified.

## 5.2.5 Consent

Guardianship is the relationship. Consent is the permission it produces, and
the two have different lifetimes and different scopes.

```txt
ConsentGrant
    id
    subject_person_id       whose data
    granted_by_person_id    the guardian, or the person themselves
    tenant_id               tenant-scoped, always
    purpose                 enumerated, not free text
    granted_at
    expires_at
    withdrawn_at            nullable
    evidence_reference      how consent was captured
```

```txt
Guardianship     global, durable, evidenced
ConsentGrant     tenant-scoped, purpose-bound, expiring, revocable
```

`purpose` is an enumeration rather than free text because consent must be
auditable against a fixed list — `PROGRAMME_PARTICIPATION`,
`PHOTOGRAPHY_AND_MEDIA`, `MEDICAL_EMERGENCY_CONTACT`,
`MARKETING_COMMUNICATION`, `THIRD_PARTY_SHARING`, and so on. Free-text
purposes cannot be reasoned about at query time and make "what has this
guardian actually agreed to?" unanswerable.

**Withdrawal is not deletion.** A withdrawn consent retains its record; it is
the evidence that processing before withdrawal was lawful.

### Consent as an authorization input

Consent participates in decisions but is not itself a relationship in the
authorization graph:

```txt
Can guardian G book programme P for minor M?
    authorization  G has guardianship over M               ← OpenFGA
    domain         active ConsentGrant, purpose =
                   PROGRAMME_PARTICIPATION, tenant = this  ← PostgreSQL
```

The relationship is in the graph; the consent is domain state — a direct
application of Principle 3.

## 5.2.6 Minors

The system contains minors by design. Junior membership categories exist for
people up to 18, so this is not an edge case.

**Rules applying wherever a Person is under the age of majority for their
jurisdiction:**

| Rule              | Effect                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Consent           | Actions requiring consent need an active `ConsentGrant` from a verified guardian                                          |
| Communication     | Communications route to the guardian unless a tenant policy permits direct contact at a configured age                    |
| Visibility        | A minor's records are visible to their guardians and to role-holders with an operational need, never broadly              |
| Data minimisation | Optional personal fields are collected only where a stated purpose exists                                                 |
| Staff contact     | Roles granting unsupervised access to minors require clearance (05.9)                                                     |
| Majority          | Reaching majority ends guardianship and its consent grants; it does **not** change the person's membership plan (ADR-048) |

Age of majority is jurisdictional and read from `Tenant.jurisdiction`, not
hard-coded to 18.

## 5.2.7 Contact points

```txt
ContactPoint
    id
    person_id
    kind                EMAIL | PHONE | POSTAL
    value
    verified_at         nullable
    is_primary
    belongs_to_guardian nullable → Person
```

`belongs_to_guardian` handles the common case where a minor's contact details
are in fact the guardian's. Recording this explicitly prevents the guardian's
email quietly becoming the minor's identity.

Verified contact points are how create-or-link works during registration
without exposing the global person directory.

## 5.2.8 Published events

```txt
PersonCreated              → Membership, Safeguarding
PersonMerged               → all contexts holding person references
PersonScrubbed             → all contexts
PrincipalCreated           → Authorization
PrincipalSuspended         → Authorization  (synchronous)
GuardianshipEstablished    → Authorization
GuardianshipEnded          → Authorization  (synchronous)
ConsentGranted             → Programme, Resource
ConsentWithdrawn           → Programme, Resource  (synchronous)
```

Guardianship ending and consent withdrawal are synchronous: the moment a
guardian loses standing, they must lose the ability to act.

## 5.2.9 Authorization model fragment

```txt
type person
  relations
    define guardian: [person]
    define self: [principal]

type principal
  relations
    define person: [person]
```

Deliberately minimal. Almost everything a person can do derives from
relationships defined in other contexts — membership, role assignment,
entitlement. This context contributes the subject, not the powers.

## 5.2.10 Data protection notes

This context is where most obligations under GDPR and India's DPDP Act
attach. Recorded here so they are visible at the point of design rather than
discovered at audit.

| Concern            | Treatment                                                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lawful basis       | Consent grants carry explicit purpose; contract and legitimate-interest bases are recorded on the membership, not here                                     |
| Special categories | Not stored in this context; constitutional eligibility criteria are tenant-configured (ADR-051) and evidenced via Verification, never as Person attributes |
| Minor's data       | Consent required, minimisation enforced, guardian-mediated by default                                                                                      |
| Right to erasure   | Implemented as `SCRUBBED`, preserving audit — see 11 for the tension with retention obligations                                                            |
| Cross-border       | Person is global within a regional instance; residency is handled by instance separation, not by row-level geography                                       |
| Purpose limitation | Enumerated purposes make this queryable rather than aspirational                                                                                           |

The `sex` field is nullable and optional. It exists because accommodation
allocation in shared hostel rooms may require it operationally. It is not
collected by default, has no authorization use, and no other context reads
it.
