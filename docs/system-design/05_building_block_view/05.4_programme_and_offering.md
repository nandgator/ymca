# 05.4 — Programme and Offering

Owns what the association offers: the catalogue, its commercial instances,
who has joined them, and when they actually happen.

Four layers that are routinely collapsed into one "class" or "activity"
table, and that collapse is what makes pricing history, schedule changes and
historical reporting intractable later.

---

## 5.4.1 Responsibilities

**Owns**

- `Programme` — the catalogue entry
- `Offering` — a specific commercial or operational instance
- `PriceComponent` — the several simultaneous prices an offering carries
- `Enrollment` — a person's participation
- `Occurrence` — a scheduled session
- `Attendance` — who actually turned up

**Does not own**

- The facility a session uses (Resource, 05.5) — an occurrence *requests*
  an allocation, it does not own the pool
- Entitlement to join (Membership, 05.3)
- Money (Finance, 05.8) — charges are raised, not settled, here

---

## 5.4.2 The four layers

```txt
PROGRAMME       what do we offer?              Swimming
      ↓
OFFERING        this instance of it            Beginner, September 2026
      ↓
ENROLLMENT      this person joined it          Alice
      ↓
OCCURRENCE      this scheduled session         Tuesday 18:00
```

Mapped to their commercial meaning:

```txt
PRODUCT / CATALOG
      ↓
COMMERCIAL OFFERING
      ↓
CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIP
      ↓
SCHEDULED DELIVERY
```

The separation buys three things that are painful to retrofit: prices that
change between batches without rewriting history, schedules that shift
without touching enrollments, and the ability to answer "what did this cost
in 2026" years later.

Note that `Occurrence` hangs off `Offering`, not off `Enrollment`. A session
exists whether or not anyone attends it.

## 5.4.3 Programme

```txt
Programme
    id
    tenant_id
    org_unit_id           the unit that owns it
    name
    category              tenant-defined
    description
    status                DRAFT | ACTIVE | ARCHIVED
```

Deliberately thin. A programme carries no price, no schedule and no capacity
— all of that belongs to its offerings. It is the stable identity that
survives across years of batches.

**A programme may exist with no resource at all** (ADR-056). Online
counselling, community outreach and rural development programmes consume no
bookable facility. Programmes are therefore not resource wrappers, and any
model that makes a programme a property of a room is wrong.

`org_unit_id` is administrative ownership, which may differ from where
sessions physically happen. A physical education department may own the
swimming programme while its occurrences take place at three different
branches.

## 5.4.4 Offering

```txt
Offering
    id
    programme_id
    code
    name                     "Beginner Swimming — September 2026"
    enrollment_opens_at
    enrollment_closes_at
    starts_on
    ends_on
    capacity                 nullable; null means uncapped
    min_enrollment           nullable; below this the offering may be cancelled
    eligibility_policy_id    nullable → Policy
    status                   DRAFT | OPEN | FULL | CLOSED |
                             RUNNING | COMPLETED | CANCELLED
```

### Offerings need not be commercial

(ADR-054) An offering may carry no price at all, or require membership with
no additional fee. Free community programmes are first-class, not a price of
zero pretending to be a product.

```txt
PRICED           swimming coaching, ₹1,500 for members
MEMBERSHIP_ONLY  included in the entitlement bundle, no charge
FREE             open to the community, no membership required
```

This distinction is not cosmetic: a `FREE` offering skips the Finance
integration entirely rather than raising a zero-value invoice, and a
`MEMBERSHIP_ONLY` offering checks entitlement instead of raising a charge.

### Capacity lives here, not on the programme

Because it is a property of the batch. September's beginner swimming may take
twenty; October's may take twelve because a different pool is available.

## 5.4.5 Pricing

An offering carries several prices simultaneously (ADR-055).

```txt
PriceComponent
    id
    offering_id
    audience              MEMBER | NON_MEMBER | STUDENT_MEMBER |
                          DEPENDENT | STAFF | tenant-defined
    kind                  BASE | EARLY_BIRD | LATE | SIBLING_DISCOUNT
    amount
    currency
    valid_from            nullable
    valid_until           nullable
    availability          AVAILABLE | UNAVAILABLE
```

```txt
Swimming — September batch
    MEMBER          BASE         ₹1,500
    STUDENT_MEMBER  BASE         ₹1,200
    MEMBER          EARLY_BIRD   ₹1,200   until 2026-08-15
    NON_MEMBER                   UNAVAILABLE
```

`UNAVAILABLE` is a first-class value, distinct from absent. "Non-members may
not enrol" is a statement the offering makes, not an omission to be inferred.

### Price resolution

```txt
resolve_price(person, offering, at_time)

  1. determine audience     from Membership context
  2. filter components      matching audience and time window
  3. if any UNAVAILABLE     → not purchasable by this audience
  4. select                 lowest applicable amount
  5. add tax                via jurisdiction calculator (05.8)
```

Step 4 defaults to the lowest applicable price because that is the least
surprising behaviour for the member and the least likely to generate a
support call. It is a `DEFAULT`-level policy, so a tenant may override it.

**Resolved price is snapshotted onto the enrollment.** The offering's prices
may change afterwards; what Alice agreed to pay must not.

## 5.4.6 Enrollment

```txt
Enrollment
    id
    offering_id
    person_id                the participant
    enrolled_by_person_id    may differ — guardian, or a member
                             enrolling a guest (ADR-065)
    consent_grant_id         nullable → ConsentGrant, required for minors
    price_snapshot           amount, currency, audience, kind
    invoice_id               nullable → Finance
    state
    enrolled_at
```

### State machine

```txt
       REQUESTED
           │
     ┌─────┴─────┐
     ▼           ▼
 WAITLISTED   CONFIRMED ──────▶ COMPLETED
     │           │
     │ promoted  │ cancel
     └──────────▶│
                 ▼
             CANCELLED
```

`WAITLISTED` exists as a status now; promotion, position and notification are
deferred (ADR-062). A full offering returns "would waitlist" rather than a
hard rejection, so the feature attaches later without schema change.

### Actor and beneficiary differ

`person_id` is who participates. `enrolled_by_person_id` is who acted. They
diverge constantly — a guardian enrolling a child, a member enrolling a
guest — and both must be recorded.

For minors, `consent_grant_id` is mandatory and must be active, tenant-scoped
and of purpose `PROGRAMME_PARTICIPATION`. The authorization graph confirms
the guardianship relationship; the consent is domain state checked here.

### Eligibility versus authorization

```txt
AUTHORIZATION   Alice is entitled to enrol in this programme
                (membership bundle, subscription, or open offering)

DOMAIN          enrollment window open?
                capacity remaining?
                eligibility policy satisfied (age band, prerequisites)?
                consent present, if a minor?
                arrears suspension absent?
```

Principle 3, at this seam. The first is one `check()` call; the rest are
domain state, none of which belongs in the authorization graph.

## 5.4.7 Occurrence

```txt
Occurrence
    id
    offering_id
    starts_at
    ends_at
    allocation_id       nullable → Allocation (05.5)
    instructor_person_id nullable
    status              SCHEDULED | RUNNING | COMPLETED |
                        CANCELLED | RESCHEDULED
    superseded_by_id    nullable → Occurrence
```

### Occurrences request allocations; they do not own resources

```txt
Occurrence(Tue 18:00)
    ──requests──▶ Allocation(pool, 18:00–19:00)
```

If the allocation cannot be made — the pool is already allocated — the
occurrence cannot be scheduled. This routes through the same exclusivity
constraint as bookings and stays (05.5), which is precisely why that
constraint lives in the database rather than in any one aggregate.

An occurrence may have **no** allocation. Online counselling occupies no
room.

### Instructors need clearance

`instructor_person_id` is not merely informational. Where a programme is
flagged as youth-facing, assigning an instructor requires an active clearance
(05.9), and an expired clearance renders the assignment inert at decision
time — the same mechanism as an expired term (ADR-070).

### Rescheduling preserves history

A moved session is a new `Occurrence` with `superseded_by_id` pointing back,
not an update to `starts_at`. Attendance already recorded against the
original must remain attached to the session that actually happened.

## 5.4.8 Attendance

```txt
Attendance
    id
    occurrence_id
    person_id
    state           PRESENT | ABSENT | EXCUSED | LATE
    recorded_by
    recorded_at
```

Thin by design. Attendance is a record, not a workflow. It matters for
safeguarding (who was with which children, when), for programme reporting,
and for prerequisite checking on future offerings.

Note it references `Occurrence` and `Person` directly rather than
`Enrollment` — a person may attend a session they were not enrolled in
(a trial, a substitute), and that fact must be recordable.

## 5.4.9 Published events

```txt
OfferingOpened            → Authorization
OfferingClosed            → Authorization
OfferingCancelled         → Finance, Resource  (releases allocations)
EnrollmentConfirmed       → Finance, Authorization
EnrollmentCancelled       → Finance, Authorization  (synchronous)
OccurrenceScheduled       → Resource  (requests allocation)
OccurrenceCancelled       → Resource  (releases allocation)
InstructorAssigned        → Safeguarding  (clearance check)
AttendanceRecorded        → (no downstream consumer yet)
```

## 5.4.10 Authorization model fragment

```txt
type programme
  relations
    define tenant: [tenant]
    define org_unit: [organizational_unit]
    define manager: [person] or admin from org_unit
    define instructor: [person]

type offering
  relations
    define programme: [programme]
    define manager: manager from programme
    define enrollable: entitled from programme
```

Enrollments themselves are **not** FGA tuples. An enrollment is a business
fact with dates, price and state — it lives in PostgreSQL. What lives in the
graph is the entitlement that made it permissible, and the management
authority over the offering.

This is the clearest illustration of ADR-024: the authorization graph knows
that Alice *may* enrol, not that she *did*.

## 5.4.12 Open items

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| Prerequisites | Referenced in eligibility; the prerequisite model (completed offering, assessed level) is not designed |
| Recurring schedule generation | Occurrences are modelled individually; the rule that generates "every Tuesday for twelve weeks" is unspecified |
| Instructor scheduling conflicts | The allocation constraint protects resources, not people; double-booking an instructor is currently possible |
| Waitlist promotion | Deferred by ADR-062; the seam is `EnrollmentCancelled` |
