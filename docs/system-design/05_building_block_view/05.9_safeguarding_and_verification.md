# 05.9 — Safeguarding and Verification

Owns the record that something was checked and the consequences that follow:
verifications, clearances, and restrictions on what a person may do.

The smallest context by surface area and the one with the strictest rules
about what it may hold. Principle 5 governs everything here:

> **Store the verdict, not the evidence.**

---

## 5.9.1 Responsibilities

**Owns**

- `Verification` — a recorded check of any kind
- `Clearance` — a verification of type background check, with validity
- `Restriction` — a bar on a person holding certain roles
- `ComplianceAttestation` — a tenant's certification against a standard
- Re-verification scheduling and expiry

**Does not own**

- Allegations, investigations, case files — **out of scope entirely**
  (ADR-089)
- The evidence behind any verification — held by the provider
- Role assignment (Authorization) — this context gates it, does not perform
  it

---

## 5.9.2 Verification

A single object serving every "was this checked?" question in the platform.

```txt
Verification
    id
    tenant_id             nullable; some verifications are global
    subject_type          PERSON | ORGANIZATION
    subject_id
    type                  IDENTITY | STUDENT_STATUS | GUARDIANSHIP
                        | BACKGROUND_CHECK | REGISTRY_SCREENING
                        | TRAINING_COMPLETION | COMPLIANCE_ATTESTATION
                        | ADDRESS | AGE
    status                PENDING | CLEAR | NOT_CLEAR | EXPIRED
                        | WITHDRAWN
    provider_code         nullable; who performed it
    evidence_reference    opaque external identifier — never content
    verified_by_person_id nullable; for manually verified types
    verified_at
    expires_at            nullable
```

### What `evidence_reference` may and may not be

```txt
MAY BE     an opaque provider case ID
           a hash of a document held elsewhere
           a URL into a provider's system requiring separate auth

MAY NOT BE the document itself
           extracted fields from the document
           a description of what the document showed
           an ID number, certificate number, or registry entry
```

The temptation to store "just the ID number so staff can check it" is exactly
what Principle 5 exists to prevent. If a check is needed, run it again
through the provider.

### Why one object rather than several

Student status, identity at hostel check-in, guardianship claims, background
clearance and compliance certification are the same shape: someone or
something was checked, by someone, at a time, with a verdict and an expiry.
Modelling them separately produces five tables with identical columns and
five re-verification mechanisms.

---

## 5.9.3 Clearance

A `Verification` of type `BACKGROUND_CHECK` or `REGISTRY_SCREENING`, with
additional lifecycle.

```txt
Clearance
    verification_id       → Verification
    person_id
    tenant_id
    scope_of_check        jurisdiction and check types performed
    valid_from
    valid_until
    next_check_due_at
    supersedes_id         nullable
```

### Clearance is recurring, not one-time

Field practice re-checks on a fixed cycle, at rehire, after a break in
service, and on transfer into a youth-facing role. The model must therefore
support **event-driven re-verification**, not just expiry.

```txt
Triggers for re-verification
    scheduled          CLEARANCE_VALIDITY_MONTHS elapsed  [Policy]
    role transfer      moving into a role requiring stricter clearance
    break in service   configurable gap in employment or volunteering
    return             rehire or re-registration
    manual             requested by a role-holder with authority
```

A `Verification` object without re-verification triggers would have been a
document upload. With them, it is a lifecycle — which is what the domain
actually requires.

### Clearance gates role assignment

```txt
RoleDefinition
    required_clearance_types []
    youth_facing              boolean
```

```txt
assign(principal, role, scope)
    if role.required_clearance_types is non-empty:
        require an active Clearance covering every type
        → otherwise the assignment is REFUSED
```

And, critically (ADR-087, mirroring ADR-070):

```txt
An expired clearance renders the assignment INERT at decision time.
```

Not revoked, not deleted — inert. The assignment row persists so that
renewing the clearance restores authority without re-appointment. But every
authorization check consults clearance validity, so a lapsed clearance
removes effect immediately, without waiting for a sweeper.

This applies to **volunteers as much as staff**. Screening obligations attach
to youth access, which is a property of the role, not of employment status.

## 5.9.4 Restriction

The only thing this context knows about a safeguarding concern.

```txt
Restriction
    id
    person_id
    tenant_id             nullable; may be platform-wide
    kind                  NO_YOUTH_FACING_ROLES
                        | NO_ROLE_ASSIGNMENT
                        | NO_RESOURCE_ACCESS
                        | SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW
    imposed_by_person_id
    imposed_at
    decision_record_id    nullable → DecisionRecord
    external_case_ref     opaque; points at the external case system
    expires_at            nullable
    lifted_at
    lifted_by_person_id
```

### What a restriction contains, and what it does not

```txt
IN     that a restriction exists
       what it prevents
       who imposed it and when
       an opaque reference to an external case

OUT    allegation details, narratives, witness accounts
       investigation notes, findings, correspondence
       the reporter's identity
       any description of what happened
```

**A restriction is an authorization fact. The case behind it is not** (ADR-089).

The reasoning: authorities are the real system of record for allegations; the
data concerns named individuals including minors; retention obligations
differ from everything else in the platform; investigation material may
attract legal privilege; and specialist tooling already exists for case
management.

`SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW` carries no reason narrative deliberately. A person
may be stood down while a matter is examined without the platform recording
an accusation.

### Access to restrictions is itself restricted

```txt
Visible to    the person themselves (that a restriction exists)
              role-holders with safeguarding.read at the relevant scope
              nobody else — not tenant admins by default
```

Restrictions do not appear in ordinary member listings, exports, or admin
views. Reading one emits an audit event.

## 5.9.5 Registry screening

Supported, **disabled by default**, enabled per tenant where the
jurisdiction supports it (ADR-090).

```txt
SCREENING_ENABLED       [Policy, OVERRIDE, per tenant]
```

Registry screening presumes registry infrastructure that does not exist in
most jurisdictions, including the largest part of the intended user base. A
platform assuming it would be unimplementable there — so it is a capability,
not a baseline.

### Screening never auto-acts

```txt
screening run
      ↓
  no match  → Verification(CLEAR), nothing else happens
      ↓
    match   → ReviewTask created
              → a human with safeguarding authority examines it
              → that human decides
              → the decision is the audit record
              → a Restriction may follow
```

(ADR-091) No automatic bar, ever. Registry matches are frequently false
positives on name collisions, and an automatic bar on a name match would
exclude innocent people with no human in the loop and no appeal path.

The screening result stores match/no-match, provider and date — never the
match content.

## 5.9.6 Compliance attestation

How safeguarding standards connect to affiliation (ADR-088).

```txt
ComplianceAttestation
    verification_id       → Verification(COMPLIANCE_ATTESTATION)
    tenant_id             who is attesting
    standard_code         the standard attested against
    required_by_org_id    who requires it (holds may_review_compliance)
    attested_by_person_id
    period_start
    period_end
    status                ATTESTED | LAPSED | WITHDRAWN | DISPUTED
```

```txt
Tenant attests annually against a safeguarding standard
        ↓
Verification(COMPLIANCE_ATTESTATION, CLEAR, expires_at)
        ↓
Attestation lapses
        ↓
Body holding may_review_compliance sees the lapse
        ↓
Body holding may_sanction MAY act — explicitly, with grounds
        ↓
Affiliation.state → SUSPENDED
```

**No parallel mechanism.** The chain runs entirely through machinery that
already exists: Verification for the attestation, the authority verbs from
05.1.5 for who may review and sanction, and the explicit-sanction rule from
ADR-074 for what happens next.

Field evidence matches this shape exactly: maintaining national affiliation
requires certifying that screening standards are followed. The design
predicted the mechanism before the instance was found, which is a reasonable
indication the abstraction is right.

Note the lapse **enables** a sanction and does not impose one. Nothing is
automatic (ADR-074).

## 5.9.7 Published events

```txt
VerificationCompleted        → the requesting context
VerificationExpiring         → notification, 60/30/7 days ahead
VerificationExpired          → Authorization  (assignments become inert)
ClearanceGranted             → Governance, Programme
ClearanceLapsed              → Authorization  (synchronous)
ReVerificationRequired       → notification, workflow
RestrictionImposed           → Authorization  (synchronous), high-severity audit
RestrictionLifted            → Authorization
ScreeningMatchFound          → safeguarding review queue only
AttestationLapsed            → Organization
```

`VerificationExpiring` matters for the same reason `OfficeTermExpiring` does:
decision-time enforcement is correct but silent. An instructor discovering
mid-session that their clearance lapsed overnight is a correct outcome and an
operationally bad one.

`ScreeningMatchFound` routes **only** to the safeguarding review queue. It
does not notify the person's manager, appear in dashboards, or emit a
general audit event visible to tenant admins.

## 5.9.8 Authorization model fragment

```txt
type restriction
  relations
    define subject: [person]
    define tenant: [tenant]
    define viewer: subject or safeguarding_reader from tenant
```

Clearances and verifications are **not** in the authorization graph. They are
business state with validity windows, consulted at decision time alongside
the graph's answer.

Putting clearance validity into OpenFGA would require rewriting tuples on a
clock — the same sweeper dependency rejected for office terms (05.6.10). The
graph says Alice holds the instructor role; PostgreSQL says whether her
clearance is current; the decision needs both.

## 5.9.9 Data protection

This context handles the most sensitive material in the platform, and its
protections are structural rather than procedural.

| Concern | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Criminal-history data | Never stored. Only verdict, provider, dates |
| Registry match content | Never stored. Only match/no-match |
| Allegation material | Out of scope entirely |
| Minor's involvement | Restrictions reference the restricted adult, never the child |
| Access | Requires explicit safeguarding permission; not implied by tenant admin |
| Audit | Every read of a restriction is audited |
| Retention | Restrictions outlive memberships; clearances expire and are superseded, not deleted |
| Cross-tenant | Restrictions may be platform-wide where a person is barred everywhere — the one place a safeguarding fact crosses a tenant boundary, and it requires platform-level authority |

That last row is a deliberate, narrow exception to Principle 1. A person
barred from youth-facing roles in one association may reasonably be barred
everywhere, and the alternative — each tenant discovering independently — is
worse. It requires platform security authority, emits a high-severity audit
event, and is the only cross-tenant safeguarding flow in the design.
